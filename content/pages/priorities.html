CHAPTER 01 — PRIORITIES

Priorities & Mindset

When everything feels urgent, the Rule of Threes tells you what actually kills first — and the manuals are unanimous that the first tool you deploy is a calm head. This chapter covers the priority order, survival psychology, and the evasion-and-recovery knowledge the military trains by.

THE LIST, NEVER UP IT
01Priorities

The Rule of ThreesWhen everything feels urgent, this tells you what actually kills first. Work down the list — never up it.

3
Minutes

Without air

Severe bleeding and icy water belong here too. They can kill faster than anything else — handle them immediately.

3
Hours

Without shelter

In harsh conditions — cold, wet, wind, or extreme heat — exposure becomes lethal before thirst or hunger do.

3
Days

Without water

Your body is ~60% water. Losing just 2% of it measurably degrades judgment, mood, and physical output.

3
Weeks

Without food

Food is the last priority. Hunger is uncomfortable, not urgent — don't burn energy chasing it early.

First skill: stop, don't sprintMost survival situations are made fatal by panic within the first hour. Before any technique, run this loop.

S

Stop

Sit down. Breathe slowly. Panic makes people abandon gear, walk in circles, and die within a day of safety.

T

Think

What do you actually have? Who knows where you are? How long until they expect you back?

O

Observe

Scan the terrain, weather, daylight remaining, water sources, and natural shelter before moving a step.

P

Plan

Decide one course of action and commit. Conserve energy — sweat is heat loss in cold and water loss in heat.

The survivor's mindset beats the survivor's gear. People with no equipment and a calm plan are rescued every year; people with full packs and panic are not. Your brain is the one tool you always have with you.

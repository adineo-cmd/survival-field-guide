03.4Water, In Depth

Water craft from the manualsWhat the source books actually say about finding, clarifying and sterilizing water — including the improvised methods that need no equipment at all.

Never drink, whatever the thirst
  • Seawater — about 4% salt; your body spends roughly 2 litres of its own water flushing the waste from every litre you drink. It kills by dehydration.
  • Urine — about 2% salt plus body wastes; the second drink is markedly worse than the first.
  • Blood — a food, not a fluid: digesting it costs more water than it gives, and it can transmit disease.
  • Alcohol — dehydrates and clouds judgment.
Just wetting your lips with polluted water can sicken you for days. The manuals are unanimous: the water that rinses your toothbrush, your utensils and your cookpot matters exactly as much as the water you drink.
Finding water — the desert signs
  • All trails lead to water. Converging game trails, trampled ground, droppings and old campfire ashes — follow the direction the trails join.
  • Birds at dawn and dusk fly fast and low to water holes; circling flocks and evening bird noise mark water nearby.
  • Extreme day/night temperature swings condense water on metal surfaces — wipe with a cloth and wring it out.
  • Porous rock fissures hold rainwater; lower tubing and siphon, or dip a cloth on a stick.
  • Heavy dew is a real source: tie rags or fine grass tufts around your ankles and walk dew-covered grass at first light, wringing the rags into a container.

Three stills, one plastic sheetStills buy water from the sun and the ground. All three come from the Army manual, with its honest numbers: expect 0.5–1 litre per 24 hours.

Vegetation bag still

Half to three-quarters fill a clear plastic bag with green leafy vegetation — never poisonous plants; their liquid is poisonous too — add a small rock, tie the mouth airtight as close to the end as possible, and lay it mouth-uphill on a sunny slope. The rock works itself to the low point; condensation collects around it. Drain, refill, repeat.

Transpiration bag

The easiest still there is: tie the clear bag over a leafy branch with a drinking tube inserted, seal the mouth tight around the limb, and weight the branch tip below the bag's mouth so water pools at the low point. One limb serves 3–5 days without harming the tree.

Belowground solar still

Dig a bowl-shaped hole 1 m across and 60 cm deep in moist soil (dry streambeds, low ground), sump a container in the centre, run tubing from container-bottom up over the lip, cover the hole with clear plastic sealed with soil, and centre a rock on the sheet. Sun drives soil moisture up; it condenses on the sheet and drips into the container.

Clarifying muddy water first
  1. 1

    Let it rest 12 hours if you can — a pinch of alum precipitates floating clay faster.

  2. 2

    Strain through closely woven cloth (a felt hat, drill trousers) or a 1 m bamboo tube packed with sand and grass at the end.

  3. 3

    Charcoal sieve clarifies and partly purifies — fire bits of charred hardwood into the filter or simmer them in the pot 10–15 minutes to sweeten swamp-tasting water.

  4. 4

    Then sterilize — filtering clears water; it does not make it safe. Boil or chemically treat everything filtered.

No fireproof container? Drop hot stones into the filtered water to bring it to a boil — the field method for sterilizing in a cloth bag or wooden trough.
Chemical dosing, from the books
AgentDose & wait
Boiling10 minutes rolling (the older manuals' conservative figure); 1 min suffices at sea level by modern guidance, 3 min above 2,000 m
Iodine tablets1 tablet (8 mg iodine) per quart, cap loosely 3 min, shake so threads are wetted, wait 10 min — 20 min if cold, 2 tablets if murky
Tincture of iodine1 drop per quart, 30 min; double dose or time if in doubt
Halazone1 tablet per pint, 2 if in serious doubt; stand 30+ minutes
Household bleach (Javel)5 drops per 4.5 litres — never exceed; tastes acidic
Chloride of limeDissolve 1 heaping tablespoon in 8 quarts; dose 1 part solution per 100 parts water; wait 30 min
Potassium permanganateOne crystal-piece until water is hardly tinted; wait 1 hour
No chemical method is as safe as boiling. Chlorine compounds fail outright in the tropics — the services standardised on tetraglycine hydroperiodide iodine there. Disinfect the bottle's threads and lip, or you recontaminate everything.
Flat-tasting boiled water has lost its air: pour it back and forth between containers, shake it half-full, or add a pinch of salt. Bad-smelling water holes: absent green vegetation and scattered bones mean dissolved poison — dig a seepage hole 3 m back from the bank and below waterline instead (Baden-Powell's trick), and boil what seeps in.

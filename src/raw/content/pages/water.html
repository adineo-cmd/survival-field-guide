Water — find it, treat itPlan on 3–4 litres per day, more in heat or at altitude. Finding water is half the job; the other half is making it safe.

Finding water
  • Rank your sources: falling rain and snow are safest, then springs, then fast-moving streams, then lakes. Stagnant, foamy, or dead-animal-adjacent water is last resort only.
  • Follow the animals. Birds circling at dawn and dusk, converging game trails, and bees all point toward water.
  • Dig at the outside bend of a dry riverbed, under the shade side of cliffs, and beneath green vegetation — damp sand means a water table close by.
  • Catch it passively: tie a clear plastic bag around a sunlit leafy branch — transpiration yields clean water over hours. Stretch a tarp into a low dish for rain and dew.
  • Wipe dew off rocks and grass with an absorbent cloth before sunrise; wring it into a container.
Clear is not clean. Giardia and other pathogens are invisible. Melting snow? Melt it rather than eating it — eating snow chills your core and speeds dehydration.
Making it safe

The gold standard — kills everything that matters.

  • Bring water to a rolling boil for 1 minute (3 minutes above 2,000 m / 6,500 ft, where water boils cooler).
  • Boiled water tastes flat because it loses oxygen — shake it hard in a closed bottle to restore taste.
  • This is why your kit needs a metal container, not just plastic bottles.

Removes particles and most bacteria and protozoa — but not all viruses.

  • Commercial filters rated 0.1–0.2 micron handle giardia and most bacteria; pair with chemical treatment where viruses are a risk.
  • Field filter — layered cloth, charcoal, sand, gravel in a cut bottle — improves water, doesn't purify it. Always boil or treat afterwards.
  • Pre-filter cloudy water through cloth first; sediment eats your filter's lifespan.

Lightweight backup that treats what filters miss.

  • Unscented household bleach: 2 drops per litre of clear water (double for cloudy), mix, wait 30 minutes. It should smell faintly of chlorine.
  • Chlorine dioxide tablets are the most effective common option — they also kill viruses and cryptosporidium over a 4-hour wait.
  • Iodine works in 30 minutes but is unsafe for pregnant women and thyroid conditions.

SODIS — solar disinfection. Free, slow, weather-dependent.

  • Fill a clear PET bottle, lay it on a dark surface in full sun for 6 hours — two full days if skies are cloudy.
  • Works only with strong sunlight and water already clear enough to read a newspaper through.
  • For salt water or chemically contaminated water, only distillation (a solar still) makes it drinkable.

00First Principle

The Rule of ThreesWhen everything feels urgent, this tells you what actually kills first. Work down the list — never up it. It opens every survival manual ever written, because it decides every decision that follows.

3
Minutes

Without air

Severe bleeding and icy water belong here too. They can kill faster than anything else — handle them immediately.
3
Hours

Without shelter

In harsh conditions — cold, wet, wind, or extreme heat — exposure becomes lethal before thirst or hunger do.
3
Days

Without water

Your body is ~60% water. Losing just 2% of it measurably degrades judgment, mood, and physical output.
3
Weeks

Without food

Food is the last priority. Hunger is uncomfortable, not urgent — don't burn energy chasing it early.

＋Provenance

Distilled, not inventedEvery technique on this site traces back to a printed source — mostly public-domain military manuals and classic bushcraft texts, read in full and compressed into what actually works.

The heavyweight sources include the US Army FM 21-76 survival manual (both editions), the Canadian Forces Arctic and Sub-Arctic Survival manual, Richard Graves' The Ten Bushcraft Books, D.C. Beard's Shelters, Shacks and Shanties, the WHO medicinal plant monographs, and the Emergency War Surgery handbook — plus 144 more on the library shelves.

How this guide was built
  • 150 books collected across seven categories — military, bushcraft, plants, medicine, knots, preparedness.
  • 129 read in full via text extraction; 21 are image-only scans, catalogued for reference.
  • ~30,000 pages distilled into fifteen chapters, keeping procedures, numbers and warnings intact.
  • Everything footnoted — each chapter ends with the manuals it was drawn from.
Old books, honest limits. Some source material is decades old. Medical and plant entries in particular must be verified against current guidance for your region before you rely on them.
